Chapter 19 · Advanced Level

Basic Trigonometry

Trigonometry answers the question: if we know one angle and one side of a right triangle, can we work out all the other sides & angles? The answer is yes — through sin, cos, and tan.

conceptThe three sides of a right triangle

Relative to the angle we are looking at (θ):

HypotenuseAlways opposite the right angle, always the longest
OppositeThe side across from the angle θ
AdjacentThe side touching the angle θ (not the hypotenuse)

SOH-CAH-TOA — the mnemonic to remember

sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse
cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse
tan θ = opposite / adjacent

SOH-CAH-TOA

Sin=Opposite/Hypotenuse, Cos=Adjacent/Hypotenuse, Tan=Opposite/Adjacent. Read the first letter of each word: S-O-H, C-A-H, T-O-A.

An example: measuring a height without climbing

The angle of elevation to the top of a pole = 60°, standing 20m away
tan(60°) = height / 20
height = 20 × tan(60°) = 20 × 1.732 ≈ 34.6m

A common mistake

Swapping the "opposite" and "adjacent" sides — both are relative to the angle being used, not fixed positions in the picture. If the known angle moves to another angle of the same triangle, which side is "opposite" and which "adjacent" changes too!

Practice on your own

1. A right triangle has opposite=3 and adjacent=4. How long is the hypotenuse? (hint: Pythagoras)

2. A 10m ladder leans at 45° to the ground. How high is the top of the ladder above the ground?

Answer 1: √(3²+4²)=√25=5
Answer 2: sin(45°)=height/10 → height=10×0.707≈7.07m

codeTrigonometric functions — watch out for radians!

The functions math::sin/cos/tan in JRG (as in most programming languages) take angles in radians, not degrees. The conversion: radians = degrees × (π/180).

A classic beginner bug

Calling math::sin(60.0) directly without converting to radians — the result is COMPLETELY WRONG, because 60 is taken as 60 radians (more than 9 full turns!), not 60°. This is one of the most common trigonometry bugs in every programming language.

Quiz 1

sin θ is defined as...

Exactly! SOH: Sin = Opposite/Hypotenuse.

Remember SOH-CAH-TOA — sin always has the hypotenuse below the line.

Quiz 2

tan θ is defined as...

Right! TOA: Tan = Opposite/Adjacent.

Tan does not involve the hypotenuse at all — check TOA again.

Quiz 3

Before calling math::sin() in JRG, an angle in degrees must be...

Exactly! radians = degrees × (π/180).

Remember the classic bug above — trig functions in code use radians, not degrees.
